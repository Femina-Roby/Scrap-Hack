Every one of these started as something someone threw away. Here is what the teams sent in after Scrap Hack, in their own words.

Power Cut Companion

Built by Muhammed Shahal N, Muhammed Sinan P and Sreehitha SD

A low-cost emergency device that gives you basic lighting and an audible alert when the power goes out.

Phantom

Built by Hana B Usman, Aasim Jasim Ajmal, Niveditaa Sunu and Tejaswini B

An FPS aim-training game with precision shooting and personalised calibration, played with a physical controller: a Nerf gun the team rebuilt from e-waste so it can play their custom arcade game.

Smart GreenhouseEcoGrow

Built by Ranjana Ranjan, Ahishahar AB, Abdul Samad C V and Adhya V R

A low-cost smart greenhouse prototype built on an Arduino Uno. A soil-moisture sensor detects dry soil and a mini pump waters the plant in short timed pulses, so no water is wasted. A DHT11 sensor tracks temperature and humidity, and a fan switches on by itself when it gets too hot. An LED and a buzzer warn the grower about heat, dry soil or a sensor fault.

Reused parts like the motor, wires and charger cut both the cost and the e-waste, making smart farming affordable for small growers.

ഒരു വെടിക്ക് മൂന്ന് പക്ഷിThree birds, one shot

Built by Amal S Kumar, Christy Basil Anil, Navaneeth P and Sivakami S

Three separate projects, all from scrap: a virtual violin, a motorised clock and a modified torch.

The violin uses a potentiometer and a rack-and-pinion mechanism to control the notes, with an Arduino Nano feeding a Python audio system. The clock uses an ESP32 and a stepper motor to drive its original gears, and syncs over NTP to keep accurate time. The torch gets an RGB light module you control over Wi-Fi from an ESP32, alongside a restored Bluetooth speaker.

The team says their website is the best way in, with a few surprises along the way.

Scrap Desktop Bot

Built by Adarsh V, Liviya Elizabeth Willy, Godly K Mathews and Annu James

A desktop bot made from scrap. An OLED screen left over from old projects is driven by an STM32. An old board with an aux port connects a speaker that had sat unused for years, and the body is cardboard.

A Meta Llama 3.2 3B model runs locally on a laptop through Ollama, and an ESP32 carries the messages between the laptop and the bot. You type into the terminal, and the bot answers through its speaker.

മിന്നിയാ മിന്നിMinniya Minni

Built by Chandhana Saji, Gowripriya M J and Gibin Binoi

A retro snake game built on an ESP32 with an OLED display and a joystick, programmed in the Arduino IDE. Steer the snake, collect food and chase a higher score. A buzzer beeps when you lose.

The casing is scrap too: an old laptop charger casing holds the joystick, and an alarm clock casing houses the buzzer. Old scraps, a new game, and a second life for things that might otherwise be thrown away.
