Who am I

i'm femina. i work as a project coordinator at tinkerhub foundation. most days, i spend my time talking to campus leads and wondering what is the next big thing we can do for these students.

i myself am a complete beginner when it comes to hardware (barely know what jumper wires are). and as someone with zero ball knowledge in hardware, why would i choose to conduct a hardware hackathon? good question. the literal answer is, why not. i wanted to do something of my own, but i had no idea. then came *drumroll* Arundhathi Krishna.

smooth transition into the next section ↓

Why Scrap Hack

so there i was, wanting to do something but nothing to do. so then, this powerhouse of ideas comes up to me and says, “Why don't you run Scrap Hack?” yessss! of course, i'm up for it, i'm for anything. naturally, it came under my ownership. i gotta admit, this idea belonged to *another drumroll* Shan (Space Manager, TinkerSpace Kochi).

the whole point of Scrap Hack is simple: get people familiarized with hardware. basic sensors, breadboards, microcontrollers, etc.

although, there was a catch. you'd have to source your components from scrap shops, broken electronics, and discarded items.

The beginning

we announced scrap hack and opened registrations on oct 10, and closed them on oct 13. 121 of you got in across kochi and calicut. i then formed the teams by mapping everyone geographically and putting 3-4 members in each. every team was added to a whatsapp group, which is where most of our communication happened.

The first briefing

on oct 14, we had the first briefing. what exactly is scrap hack? what do we want you to have done by the end of the build-a-thon? what should you be doing for the next two weeks? all doubts, concerns and clarifications were sorted here.

The next two weeks

scrap diary, scrap shops and pre-event learning

through the next two weeks, you were asked to film your visits to scrap shops, and to break apart old broken things at home to see which parts could be reused. meanwhile, shan and i began our own journey. scrap shop after scrap shop, we realised this mission was harder than it looked. we saw aluminium pipes, fans, gas stoves and much more. slowly, ideas started popping into our heads.

The sessions

there were a total of 3 pre-learning sessions.

  1. Oct 18intro to hardware by Ananthu Krishna AS

    when you break something open, what are those green boards? why does everything look unfamiliar?

  2. Oct 19testing with multimeters and what next by Rio Roy

    you’ve broken stuff and found the components inside. how do you know they work?

  3. Oct 22from scrap to working prototype by Rishi Krishna

    once you know the components and how they work, how do you ideate? how do you build something out of them?

The mentors

a hackathon like this only works if the people helping you know what they’re walking into. so a few days before build day, we got the mentors on a call. we walked them through how scrap hack works, what we expected from them, the guidelines to follow through the night, and the timings for each venue: kochi from 2:00 pm on oct 24 to 10:00 am on oct 25, and calicut from 6:00 pm to 6:00 am.

Build day

habeeb looked after tinkerspace calicut. i was at tinkerspace kochi. both calicut and kochi produced cool, out of the box projects.

kochi was fun. everybody was active, and a lot of you went to scrap shops and sourced on the day itself. you found nerf guns, a kit kat stand, printers, an old telephone, mixies, kettles and more. it turned out way better than i expected.

i was also trying to build my own project with shan, and it failed completely. we’re not giving up though. we’ll keep building it, and we’ll put it out soon.

if we’d had a little more time, i think you would have come up with even cooler things. but what you built is already really, really cool.

go check out everything you made on the projects page.

Before you go

when scrap hack was planned, it worked solely on one idea: don’t begin with the idea, begin with the scrap.

a few weeks later, i was standing in tinkerspace kochi watching a kit kat stand, an old telephone and a pile of mixies turn into things i couldn’t have planned.

i didn’t expect it to turn out this well. i learned a lot, and i got a lot wrong, but i’d run it again tomorrow.

this isn’t the end of it though. i’m asking you to do four things. send me your projects, so they can go on the projects page. post your scrap diary, with tinkerhub as a collaborator. write up your build on hackster or medium. and take scrap hack to your own campus. i’ll be opening up how we ran it, so you can.

thank you for opening things up, and for not being scared of what was inside.
